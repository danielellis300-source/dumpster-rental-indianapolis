Weight Limits Explained: What Happens If You Overfill a Dumpster

March 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Weight, not volume, is the number one source of surprise fees on a dumpster rental. A container that looks far from full by volume can already be at its tonnage limit if the material is dense — concrete, dirt, and shingles are the usual culprits.

Typical included tonnage by size

  • 10-yard: around 1–2 tons included
  • 20-yard: around 2–3 tons included
  • 30-yard: around 3–4 tons included
  • 40-yard: around 4–5 tons included
These are typical allowances — confirm the exact tonnage in your quote, since it can vary by material type and local disposal rates.

Why light-looking loads can still be heavy

A dumpster half-full of shingles or broken concrete can already weigh more than one completely full of furniture and cardboard. If your project involves roofing, concrete, brick, tile, or dirt, mention it when booking — we may recommend a different size specifically to manage weight rather than volume.

What overage fees typically look like

Going over the included tonnage is billed per additional ton at a rate disclosed in your quote — typically somewhere in the $50–$100+ per ton range depending on material. On a heavy job like a full tear-off roof, this can add up fast if you didn’t size correctly from the start.

How to avoid an overage surcharge

Tell us the material type and rough quantity when booking, not just “a lot of stuff.” For known-heavy jobs like concrete removal or a roof tear-off, we’ll often recommend a size specifically calibrated for weight rather than defaulting to what fits visually.
